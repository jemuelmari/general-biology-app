📖 Plant Organ Systems — Detailed

Competency: STEM_BIO11/12-IVa-h-1

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Describe the shoot and root systems in plants.
  • Explain how xylem and phloem transport materials.
  • Describe the dermal system and its protective role.
  • Compare plant structural adaptations to those of animals.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Shoot system — above-ground organs (stems, leaves, flowers).
  • Root system — below-ground organs (roots, root hairs).
  • Vascular tissue — xylem (water) and phloem (sugars).
  • Dermal tissue — outer protective layer.

📚 Lesson Content

A. Shoot System

OrganFunctionKey Adaptations
LeavesPhotosynthesis, gas exchangeBroad surface; stomata; cuticle
StemsSupport, transportVascular bundles; nodes & internodes
FlowersReproductionAttract pollinators; produce seeds

B. Root System

  • Taproot: One main root (e.g., carrots)
  • Fibrous roots: Many thin roots (e.g., grasses)
  • Root hairs: Increase surface area for water and mineral absorption
  • Functions: Anchor, absorb, store food

C. Vascular Tissue — Xylem & Phloem

TissueTransportsDirectionCells
XylemWater and dissolved mineralsRoots → shoots (upward)Tracheids & vessel elements (dead at maturity)
PhloemSugars (sucrose) and organic nutrientsSource → sink (both directions)Sieve tubes & companion cells (living)

D. Dermal Tissue

  • Epidermis: Outer layer of cells
  • Cuticle: Waxy layer that reduces water loss
  • Stomata: Pores for gas exchange; regulated by guard cells
  • Trichomes: Hair-like structures for defense and water retention

E. Plant Adaptations vs Animal Adaptations

FunctionPlant AdaptationAnimal Adaptation
Gas exchangeStomata on leavesLungs / gills
TransportXylem & phloemHeart & blood vessels
SupportCell walls (cellulose), turgor pressureSkeletal system
Water balanceCuticle, stomatal controlKidneys, osmoregulation

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Plants have shoot and root systems.
  • Xylem transports water up; phloem transports sugars both ways.
  • The dermal system protects and controls gas exchange.
  • Plant adaptations parallel animal adaptations for the same life functions.
