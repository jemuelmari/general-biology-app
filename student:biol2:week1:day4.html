📖 Comparative Processes — Reproduction, Nutrition, Gas Exchange

Competency: STEM_BIO11/12-IVa-h-1

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Compare reproduction strategies in plants and animals.
  • Compare nutrition modes in plants and animals.
  • Compare gas exchange mechanisms in plants and animals.
  • Explain how each adaptation supports survival in each group's environment.

🔑 Key Concepts

  • Sexual vs asexual reproduction — genetic variation vs rapid multiplication.
  • Autotroph vs heterotroph — make own food vs consume others.
  • Diffusion surface — a thin, moist, large surface for gas exchange.

📚 Lesson Content

A. Reproduction

AspectAnimalsPlants
SexualGametes (sperm + egg) → zygotePollination → seed formation
AsexualBudding, fragmentation, parthenogenesisRunners, tubers, bulbs, cuttings
Advantage of sexualGenetic diversity → adaptation
Advantage of asexualFast, no mate required

B. Nutrition

AspectAnimalsPlants
ModeHeterotrophicAutotrophic (photosynthesis)
Source of carbonOrganic foodCO₂ from air
Energy sourceChemical energy from foodLight energy from sun
Key structuresMouth, digestive systemLeaves, chloroplasts, roots

C. Gas Exchange

AspectAnimalsPlants
OrgansLungs, gills, skinStomata, lenticels
O₂ intakeActive (breathing)Passive (diffusion)
CO₂ releaseExhaleDiffuse out through stomata
Special featuresAlveoli increase surface areaStomata open/close via guard cells

D. Why These Differences?

  • Animals are motile → need active systems (breathing, circulation).
  • Plants are sessile → rely on diffusion and environmental factors.
  • Each group's structures reflect its environmental niche.

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Plants and animals perform the same life processes but with different structures.
  • Animals use active systems; plants rely on diffusion.
  • Reproduction can be sexual or asexual in both groups.
  • Nutrition: animals are heterotrophs; most plants are autotrophs.
